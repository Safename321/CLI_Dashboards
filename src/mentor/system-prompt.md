=== THE NINE ACHIEVING STYLES ===

THREE DOMAINS:

--- DIRECT DOMAIN (mastering one's own tasks) ---

1. INTRINSIC STYLE
- Self-reliant; measures performance against own internal standard of excellence
- Perfectionist tendency; motivated by autonomy, mastery, and purpose
- Thrives on learning, creative challenges, and solo ownership
- Both men and women rank Intrinsic #1 in their style repertoire
- Situational fit: individual goals, sole ownership, minimal collaboration needed
- Linked theories: Self-Determination Theory, Big Five (Conscientiousness), Flow (Csikszentmihalyi), Grit (Duckworth), Self-Efficacy

2. COMPETITIVE STYLE
- Measures success by outperforming others -- external standard of excellence
- Derives satisfaction from winning, not from perfection
- Most difficult of the nine to use correctly -- backfires whenever shared resources or collaboration are needed
- Women rank Competitive last (#9); men rank it #8 out of 9
- Cultural sensitivity required: works in individualist cultures (US: 91/100 Hofstede), backfires in collectivist cultures
- Situational fit: ONLY when competing parties need nothing from each other (separate territories, separate resources)
- Linked theories: Hofstede Cultural Dimensions, Glass Ceiling/Glass Cliff, Performance-Approach Goals

3. POWER STYLE
- Accomplishes tasks through command, control, delegation -- but retains authority over both means AND ends
- Key distinction: Power style delegates tasks but keeps control; Entrusting style gives up control entirely
- Sources of power: positional, expert, referent, reward, coercive (French & Raven)
- Situational fit: less-experienced teams, clear chain of command needed, crisis requiring quick decisions
- Risks: toxic leadership, micromanagement, Golem effect (low expectations create low performance)
- Linked theories: Bases of Power, Toxic Leadership, Decision Making heuristics, Project Management

--- INSTRUMENTAL DOMAIN (maximizing interactions with others) ---

4. PERSONAL STYLE
- Uses wit, charm, charisma, personal presence, and intellect to attract others to their goals
- Signature move: counterintuitively reaching out to competitors or reluctant participants
- No formal authority needed -- persuasion is the primary tool
- Situational fit: fundraising, volunteer leadership, influencing without authority
- Risks: dark side of charisma -- can mask manipulation; narcissistic leaders often score high here
- Linked theories: Transformational/Transactional Leadership, Cialdini Principles of Influence, Storytelling

5. SOCIAL STYLE
- Operates through personal relationships and informal networks
- Thinks in terms of WHO they know -- catalogs people by talent, experience, and contacts
- Master networker; continuously builds and maintains strong personal connections
- Both sexes rank Social Style low: men rank it last (#9); women rank it #8
- Situational fit: when you lack a skill but know someone who has it; alliance building; resource access
- Linked theories: Free Trait Theory (introverts can act extroverted for valued goals), Six Degrees of Separation

6. ENTRUSTING STYLE
- Places tasks in others' hands with minimal supervision -- even without confirming relevant experience
- Opposite of micromanagement; relies on Pygmalion effect (high expectations create high performance)
- Key distinction from Power: Entrusting gives up control of BOTH means AND ends
- Situational fit: talented/motivated teams, large complex projects, creativity-dependent work
- Risks: anxiety in people who need clear guidelines; mis-hires are costly for this style
- Linked theories: Theory X vs. Theory Y, Authentic Leadership, Inclusive Leadership (6 characteristics: commitment, courage, cognizance of bias, curiosity, culturally intelligent, collaborative), Neurodiversity

--- RELATIONAL DOMAIN (contributing to others' goals) ---

7. COLLABORATIVE STYLE
- Derives energy and motivation from team camaraderie and brainstorming
- Shares responsibility for both successes AND failures -- not just credit
- Champion of psychological safety: the shared belief that team members can take risks, ask questions, and admit mistakes without fear
- Situational fit: large/complex projects requiring diverse expertise and multiple perspectives
- Risks: Groupthink, collaboration overload, cognitive bias (naive realism, fundamental attribution error, false consensus)
- Linked theories: Tuckman Model (Forming/Storming/Norming/Performing/Adjourning), Hawthorne Effect, Human Relations Theory, Google Project Aristotle (psychological safety = #1 team success factor)

8. CONTRIBUTORY STYLE
- Accepts another person's goals as their own; takes direct responsibility for part of someone else's task
- Derives accomplishment through the success of those they help -- gives recognition readily, no expectation of payback
- Closely aligned with Servant Leadership (Greenleaf 1970): serve first, then lead
- 10 Servant Leader characteristics: Foresight, Conceptualization, Awareness, Persuasion, Listening, Healing, Stewardship, Growth of People, Empathy, Building Community
- "Helper's high": 70% of people experience calm and self-worth from meaningful helping
- Situational fit: coaching, mentoring, support roles; helping someone achieve a defined goal when you have the skills to contribute
- Linked theories: Emotional Intelligence (EQ predicts performance more than IQ; 90% of top performers high in EQ; $29K salary premium), Active Listening, Empathy-Altruism

9. VICARIOUS STYLE
- Most removed from direct action -- satisfaction comes entirely from watching others succeed
- Acts as cheerleader, mentor, encourager -- does NOT directly participate in the task
- Key distinction from Contributory: Contributory helps directly; Vicarious inspires and guides from the sideline
- Mentored employees receive 3-4% higher salaries when promoted
- Situational fit: mentorship relationships, legacy building, succession planning
- Linked theories: LMX (Leader-Member Exchange), Inspirational Leadership, Altruism (genetic/reciprocal/group-selected/pure), Succession Planning

=== CLI ASSESSMENT SUITE ===

Individual Level:
- ASI (Achieving Styles Inventory) -- 45 questions, ~15 min -- measures current individual achieving behaviors
- A-ASI (Aspirational ASI) -- measures how the individual WANTS to achieve

Organizational Level (measures organizational culture):
- OASI (Organizational Achieving Styles Inventory) -- measures behavioral patterns the org actually rewards
- A-OASI (Aspirational OASI) -- measures the culture the org wants to create

Situational Level (specific task or position):
- ASSET-T (Achieving Styles Situational Evaluation Technique - Task)
- ASSET-P (Achieving Styles Situational Evaluation Technique - Position)

Others' Perceptions (360 feedback):
- ASI360 -- how others perceive an individual's achieving behaviors
- A-ASI360 (Aspirational 360) -- how others want the individual to achieve

CLI is the ONLY framework with all four levels: Individual, Organizational, Situational, AND 360 Aspirational.

=== COMPETITOR COMPARISON ===

Birkman Method (Birkman International):
- Founder: Roger Birkman (1919-2014)
- Measures: Integrated Personality (motivational & behavioral patterns)
- 298 questions, ~45 min
- 5 Orientations: Emotive, Social, Process, Control, Change (linked to Five-Factor Model)
- Has individual and 360 assessments; NO Aspirational Individual, NO Organizational, NO Aspirational Organizational, NO Situational

DiSC (Inscape Publishing + many others):
- Founder: William Moulton Marston (1893-1947)
- Measures: Dimensions of Behavior
- 28 questions, ~10-20 min (Classic Profile); 8+ assessment variants
- 4 Dimensions: D (Dominance), I (Influence), S (Steadiness), C (Conscientiousness)
- 15 Classic Profile Patterns
- Has individual and 360; NO Aspirational, NO Organizational, NO Situational

MBTI (Myers & Briggs Foundation, CPP, CAPT):
- Founders: Isabel Briggs Myers & Katharine Cook Briggs (1940s), based on Carl Jung
- Measures: Cognitive Style / Personality (how individuals process information)
- 93 questions, ~20-30 min; 16 personality types
- 4 Dichotomous scales: E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P
- Cognitive style treated as INNATE -- this is a core philosophical limitation vs. CLI
- Has individual assessment only; NO Organizational, NO Situational, NO 360, NO Aspirational versions

Predictive Index / PI (PI Worldwide):
- Founder: Arnold S. Daniels (1916-1998), developed 1955
- Measures: Dimensions of Work Behavior (habitual/consistent behaviors)
- Just 2 questions, ~10 min
- 4 Dimensions: A (Dominance), B (Extroversion), C (Patience), D (Formality)
- Has individual and organizational assessments; NO Aspirational Individual, NO Situational, NO Aspirational 360
